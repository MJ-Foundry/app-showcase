Minesweeper, the way it was always played, on a keyboard that is nicer to
press than the one you have. The board is a pink case full of cream and
blush keycaps; each one goes down with a thock when you tap it and sinks
into the case to show its number. A mine you have found gets a cherry
blossom.

Four board sizes stand in for the usual beginner-to-expert ladder, all of
them tall rather than wide so they fit a phone held in one hand. The first
tap always opens a field, numbers count the mines touching that tile, and
tapping a number whose mines are all marked opens the rest around it. Pinch
to zoom on the big boards.

Win, and a wave rolls out from your last tap and puts a key back on every
tile, with blossoms on the mines. Lose, and the keys go down in a wave from
the one that went off, showing where the mines were. Either way the board
stays in view, so you can see what happened before you go again.

The sound is a mechanical keyboard with soft switches, synthesised in the
app — no recordings. Everything stays on the device: best times, settings.
There is no account, no sync, no analytics, and no ads.
