Somewhere out over the edge of a wooden block, a small bird is waiting for a
place to sit. You have a handful of wooden sticks and nothing to fix them
with, only gravity and friction.

Put a finger down and drag, and a stick grows from where you started to where
you let go, so you choose both its length and its angle. Let go and it settles
the way a real stick would: lie it far enough back and it stays, push it too
far out and it tips into the clouds below. Stack the next one on top, a little
further out, or weigh the back end down first, and the structure reaches
further than it looks like it should. When a stick lies still beneath the
bird, it hops down and perches.

Each world adds one new idea. Pegs that hold a lever down or carry a shelf in
mid-air, stones to use as counterweights, ice where a leaning stick needs
something to rest its foot against, low cave roofs, two birds that both need
a perch, short bamboo sticks, and a heavy owl that presses down on whatever it
lands on.

Nothing is ever lost. A stick that falls gives its length back, undo is always
free, and the stars only ask how little wood you needed. The game works
offline, needs no account and collects nothing. Your stars are saved on the
device.
