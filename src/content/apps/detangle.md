A handful of rope lies in a wooden tray, crossed and woven the way rope ends up
when it has been dropped in a heap. Some of it lies on top, some of it is caught
underneath, a few strands are wound round the wooden pegs, and here and there a
rope has been tied into a knot.

Pull a rope and it moves like rope. The rest of it follows, it drags against the
wood and against the ropes lying on it, and it settles where you leave it. A rope
with nothing on top of it and no knot in it is free: it rises a little from the
pile, and you pick it up and toss it out of the tray. Often that frees the next
one too, and the tangle comes apart one rope after another.

The knots are tied in the rope itself, overhand knots and figure-eights, some
loose and some pulled snug, and they come undone the way they do in your hands:
open the loop and draw the end back through. A loop on its own is not a knot, so
a rope with only loops in it can always be lifted away.

The tray is your phone. Tilt it and the ropes slide toward the low side, give it
a small shake and the pile loosens. Tilt can be switched off for the bus.

The first piles are two or three ropes and teach one thing at a time. Later
there is more rope, more knots and longer chains of ropes holding each other
down, with a lighter level now and then to breathe and, every so often, a big
basket of rope to work through. When you are stuck, the bulb shows a good pull
with a ghost finger.

The game works offline, needs no account and collects nothing. Your progress is
saved on the device.
